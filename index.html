1. Tell me about yourself

  • International commercial career → premium / distribution → B2B technology → international new business.
  • Full-cycle operator → target → outreach → discovery → demo → proposal → negotiation → signature.
  • Leadership added over time → team building + CEO / acquisition mandate, but still happiest close to customers and revenue.
Commercial builder who still sells.

2. Why Surikat now?

  • Proven product + profitable business.
  • Commercial whitespace → no mature sales organisation; historically inbound-led.
  • Real mandate → sell personally + build process/team + work directly with CEO, board and Bridgepoint.
Strong product → blank commercial canvas → ambitious growth.

3. Why leave a CEO role?

  • CEO was a mandate → stabilise ArrivalGuides after Covid → restore value → prepare / complete strategic sale.
  • Not my desired permanent direction → whole-business administration is less motivating than commercial creation.
  • Next move deliberately returns to sell + build, with leadership scope but customer contact retained.

4. Have you really built a sales organisation?

  • Do not overclaim Precisely → that proves outbound / process creation, not people leadership.
  • Padani → recruited / developed a small team, set standards, worked alongside them; turnover nearly doubled in year one.
  • Theo Fennell → led an experienced team; direction, standards, performance.
  • CEO experience → adds senior commercial judgement and organisational context.
Separate the evidence: Precisely = PROCESS. Padani / Theo = PEOPLE.

5. What does player-coach mean to you?

  • Carry important opportunities personally.
  • Turn what works into a repeatable method.
  • Recruit and coach through real deals, not just dashboards.
  • Gradually shift the mix as the team gains capability.
Do it → prove it → teach it → scale it.

6. What would you do in the first 90 days?

  • Learn → product, customer workflows, current accounts, why inbound converts, lost deals, market priorities.
  • Map → ICP / segments / buyers / sales cycle / current pipeline / pricing / decision process.
  • Start selling early → do not spend a quarter designing slides and process.
  • Build only what the evidence requires → stages, qualification, CRM hygiene, core KPIs, first hiring profile.

7. How would you turn inbound strength into outbound growth?

  • Reverse-engineer best customers → why did they buy, which triggers, which roles, which problems?
  • Build target-account hypotheses rather than mass activity.
  • Personalised outreach + events / trade ecosystem → open doors with relevance.
  • Measure conversion quality, not email volume.
Use inbound success as data for outbound targeting.

8. How do you set KPIs without creating bureaucracy?

  • KPIs must change behaviour or improve decisions.
  • Leading: qualified pipeline created, right stakeholder engaged, next-step discipline.
  • Lagging: win rate, cycle length, new revenue / ARR, expansion.
  • Avoid vanity metrics that reward activity without commercial progress.
Measure the customer journey, not salesperson busyness.

9. How do you forecast?

  • Forecast = evidence, not seller optimism.
  • Real problem + decision-maker + budget / path + agreed next step + decision timing.
  • Stalled = challenged; “likes us” is not the same as “buying”.
  • Keep process light enough that salespeople maintain it accurately.

10. Complex / long-cycle deals?

  • Comfortable with multiple stakeholders → commercial, user, technical, procurement, legal.
  • Maintain momentum by making the next decision explicit.
  • Protect leverage → do not discount before diagnosing the real objection.
  • Keep enough pipeline not to become emotionally dependent on one big deal.

11. No maritime / terminal background?

  • Correct — say it plainly.
  • Pattern of entering new sectors → travel tech, legal SaaS and other B2B environments.
  • Learn the operation before pretending to know it → customer workflow → economic / operational problem → product value.
  • Use internal experts for technical depth; remain commercially accountable.

12. Why you over a sector specialist?

  • They may have faster day-one domain fluency.
  • You bring the transformation Surikat actually needs → international new business + process creation + people development + leadership.
  • Fresh commercial pattern recognition from several sectors can be useful when moving from inbound success to proactive scale.

13. How do you work with a PE-backed board?

  • Translate commercial reality into decision-useful information.
  • Be honest about pipeline quality, pace and constraints.
  • Connect investment to growth logic → headcount / marketing / systems must have a reason.
  • Protect the sales team from reporting theatre while giving the board real visibility.

14. What motivates you?

  • Create → new customers, new opportunities, new systems that work.
  • Solve → take something informal and make it more effective.
  • Learn → understand a new product / market deeply enough to sell it simply.
  • Surikat combines all three with visible impact.

15. Compensation / availability?

  • Already disclosed: ~SEK 70k/month base + perks + commission.
  • Notice: technically 3 months, but likely flexible with Smartvel.
  • Do not renegotiate yourself downward in a future conversation; first understand Surikat's total package, variable structure and co-investment opportunity.

These are now questions for Surikat itself, not generic recruiter questions. Prioritise the ones that reveal the actual growth model, authority, resources and operating culture.

1. What does “triple revenue” actually mean?

  • Over what time horizon?
  • How much is expected from new logos vs expansion?
  • Which geographies / segments matter most?
Understand the maths behind the ambition.

2. Why has inbound worked so well?

  • Where do leads come from today?
  • Which product / customer problem pulls them in?
  • What do the best customers have in common?
Their inbound history is the dataset for your outbound strategy.

3. What exactly am I inheriting?

  • Current pipeline? CRM? marketing support?
  • Who sells today — Andreas, founders, product leaders?
  • Existing account / customer-success resource?

4. First hires and budget?

  • When would they expect the first sales hire?
  • Which roles first: AE, BDR, account, marketing / ops?
  • Is headcount already budgeted or must growth fund it?

5. What does success look like after 12 months?

  • Revenue / logos?
  • Qualified pipeline?
  • Repeatable process?
  • Team hired?
  • Forecast confidence?
Force prioritisation: what matters most?

6. How much authority comes with the mandate?

  • Hiring / team design?
  • Pricing and discounting?
  • CRM / methodology?
  • GTM / market prioritisation?
Responsibility without authority is a warning sign.

7. How does Bridgepoint work with management?

  • Which commercial KPIs reach the board?
  • How often?
  • How involved are they in targets / hiring / GTM?
Clarify whether “discipline” means visibility or micromanagement.

8. What are typical deals?

  • Contract value / recurring mix?
  • Average sales cycle?
  • Buyer and technical stakeholders?
  • Implementation / procurement complexity?

9. What would Andreas want to stop doing?

  • Which sales activities currently sit with the CEO?
  • Which relationships should remain CEO-led?
  • What does he most want the Sales Lead to take ownership of?

10. Where do you expect this person to challenge you?

  • What commercial habits should change?
  • What assumptions are open to challenge?
  • What would be politically difficult to change?
A strong mandate should tolerate constructive challenge.

11. What is the intended player / coach mix?

  • First 6 months?
  • After first hires?
  • Longer term?
Make sure “Sales Lead” does not become pure management too quickly.

12. Co-investment opportunity?

  • When does it become available?
  • Who is eligible?
  • How is it typically structured?
Ask later in the process, once mutual fit is established.
Best cultural-fit probe: “When you talk about KPIs and reporting, what information does the management team genuinely use to make decisions — and what does the day-to-day cadence look like for the sales team?”
